PH3601 Fabrication of Micro- and Nano-electronic Devices

Course syllabus and operational details for PH3601, AY2026-2027 Semester 1.

Course Information

Course Aims

This course aims to provide a comprehensive introduction to the fabrication technologies of micro- and nano-structured electronic devices. The fabrication techniques discussed in the course are relevant for micro- and nano-devices in the fields of semiconductor, magnetics, optoelectronics, micro- and nano-electro-mechanical systems (MEMS/NEMS), and biomedical applications. The course covers essential topics including principles, fabrication and applications. The working principles of each fabrication technique are explained in detail, and the combination of these techniques into a complete process flow for making different microdevices is discussed.

The course also aims to familiarise students with the semiconductor processing techniques employed in the advanced manufacturing industry, so that they acquire direct applied knowledge before embarking on an engineering career in the semiconductor industry.

Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

Upon successful completion of this course, students should be able to:

ILO Learning Outcome
ILO 1Identify and describe the basic building blocks of semiconductor devices.
ILO 2Present a general view of the trend of semiconductor technology development and its technological ecosystem.
ILO 3Explain the importance of contamination control and apply the relevant approach in semiconductor processing.
ILO 4Describe the techniques of optical lithography, resolution enhancement, and the working mechanism of photoresist.
ILO 5Identify the properties of non-optical lithography techniques, and apply the lithography process in building microfluidic devices.
ILO 6Describe wet and dry etching and apply the process conditions to achieve the lithography minimum feature size.
ILO 7Explain the physics of doping and apply the diffusion or ion implantation conditions.
ILO 8Design the doping concentration in bulk silicon wafer growth.
ILO 9Explain vacuum technology and design a desired vacuum condition by selecting suitable pumps and gauges.
ILO 10Describe metallic and dielectric film deposition processes and apply suitable tools to grow films.
ILO 11Use appropriate metallic layers for the interconnect metallisation process.
ILO 12Design probing configurations for device electrical measurement techniques.
ILO 13Describe the device packaging process and apply a suitable model for manufacturing yield calculation.
ILO 14Design an integrated process to complete CMOS transistor fabrication.
ILO 15Search relevant references and review technical topics.
ILO 16Write technical review reports and give a technical presentation on the surveyed topics.

Learning and Teaching Approach

Approach How It Supports the Learning Outcomes
LecturesStudents are first motivated with the relevant technology and processing techniques of electronic devices, followed by lectures that explain the working principles and physics. Wrap-up questions are provided.
TutorialsDiscussion of tutorial questions helps to consolidate understanding of the main concepts learned in lectures.
Lab DemonstrationsHeld at the SPMS/PAP Microfabrication Cleanroom Laboratory, giving students first-hand experience of device microfabrication processes. Techniques such as photolithography, physical vapour deposition and etching are demonstrated, and students are encouraged to ask questions so that the field experience reinforces the lectures.
Technology-enhanced LearningExtended learning is facilitated by online materials, including recorded lectures and videos of fabrication techniques and processes.

Assignment (CA1)

  • Conduct a literature survey on a topic relevant to micro- and nano-fabrication, or micro- and nano-electronic devices, chosen from a list of topics provided. A self-proposed topic relevant to the course objectives is also allowed.
  • Report length: 3,500 to 20,000 words.
  • Students may work in pairs on the same topic; however, each student must submit an individual report, and reports within a pair cannot be identical.
  • After report submission, each student gives a 20-minute oral presentation. A joint presentation by a pair is allowed, with 30 minutes given.
  • The report is assessed on: quality of write-up; structure and organisation; quality of information gathering and references; presentation and discussion of surveyed information; and logical analysis and conclusion.
  • The presentation is assessed on: quality of work presented; ability to give technical explanations; quality of presentation materials; clarity, language use and accuracy; and questions and answers.

Formative Feedback

  • Feedback through discussion within tutorial lessons.
  • Written and/or oral feedback on the assignment report and presentation.
  • Feedback after each term test on common mistakes and the level of difficulty of the problems.
  • Past examination questions and previous examiner's reports are discussed in lectures.

Course Policies

Academic Integrity. Students are expected to uphold the NTU Honour Code and avoid all forms of academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, academic fraud, collusion and cheating. On the use of technological tools such as Generative AI, refer to the specific assignment instructions or consult the instructor.

Attendance. Students are encouraged to attend from the start of the course. Although lectures are recorded, lab demonstrations offer first-hand experience of the systems and equipment discussed in lectures, and later lectures build on earlier content.

Absence. If you are unable to attend a class (particularly a midterm test) due to illness, email the instructor and submit the original medical certificate, issued in Singapore by a registered medical practitioner, to the administrator. A make-up test is conducted only for students absent from both midterm tests with valid reasons.

Diversity and Inclusion. The course aims to provide an inclusive and collaborative learning environment. If your learning is being affected by any circumstance, please speak to the teaching team or the school pastoral officer. All members of the class are expected to respect one another and adhere to the NTU anti-harassment policy.
