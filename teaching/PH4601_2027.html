PH4601 Physics of Semiconductor and Devices

Course syllabus and operational details for PH4601, AY2026-2027 Semester 2.

Course Information

Course Code: PH4601 Course Title: Physics of Semiconductor and Devices Academic Year: AY2026-2027, Semester 2 Academic Units: 3 AU Contact Hours: 39 Pre-requisite: PH3102 Condensed Matter Physics I or PH3103 Technological Applications of Quantum Mechanics Instructor: Lew Wen Siang Office: SPMS-PAP-03-04 Phone: 6316 2963 Email: wensiang@ntu.edu.sg Lecture/Tutorial Hours: Wednesday 11.30 am - 12.20 pm, Thursday 11.30 am - 1.20 pm Lecture Venue: SPMS-LT2 (Wednesday), and SPMS-LT3 (Thursday)

Course Aims

This course aims to provide a comprehensive introduction to the physics of semiconductors and devices. It covers essential topics including principles and design, in order to provide foundational knowledge of the functionality and applications of the devices. Students are taught to design experiments that use these devices, linking theory and practice so that the concepts learned in the course can be implemented. Widely used semiconductor devices, both logic devices such as the diode and transistor and memory devices such as SRAM, DRAM and NAND Flash, are explained in detail.

The course also aims to familiarise students with the common semiconductor devices in the advanced manufacturing industry, so that they have the relevant background before embarking on an engineering career in the semiconductor industry.

Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

Upon successful completion of this course, students should be able to:

ILO Learning Outcome
ILO 1Explain the fundamentals of semiconductor physics.
ILO 2Use extrinsic semiconductor concepts to analyse carrier concentrations in semiconductor devices.
ILO 3Explain the carrier transport phenomena in semiconductors, and apply the relevant concepts in experimental measurement to calculate transport properties such as carrier type and concentration, mobility, conductivity and total current density in semiconductor devices.
ILO 4Describe the concepts of generation and recombination of excess carriers in the non-equilibrium state of semiconductors, and apply the ambipolar transport model to determine excess carrier dynamics when a device is under external stimuli.
ILO 5Explain the working principles of the pn junction diode, and use the concepts to design device current-voltage characteristics.
ILO 6Explain the working principles of the metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor (MOSFET), and use the concepts to design device current-voltage characteristics.
ILO 7Identify the physical limitations of the MOSFET and understand the engineering approaches to transistor advancement, such as FinFET and GAA.
ILO 8Explain the concepts of semiconductor memory devices, such as SRAM, DRAM and NAND Flash.
ILO 9Explain the concepts of metal-semiconductor devices, such as the Schottky device, and use the concepts to design device current-voltage characteristics.
ILO 10Explain the concepts of the bipolar transistor and the heterostructure bipolar transistor.
ILO 11Explain the concepts of other semiconductor devices for microwave and power applications, such as MESFET, HEMT, RTD, GaN and SiC devices.

Learning and Teaching Approach

Approach How It Supports the Learning Outcomes
LecturesStudents are first motivated with the relevant technology and processing techniques of electronic devices, followed by lectures that explain the working principles and physics. Wrap-up questions are provided.
TutorialsDiscussion of tutorial questions helps to consolidate understanding of the main concepts learned in lectures.
Device SimulationStudents are introduced to Technology Computer-Aided Design (TCAD) tools for simulating and optimising semiconductor devices. Lab tutors conduct software simulation sessions so that students gain the understanding to design and simulate semiconductor devices.
Technology-enhanced LearningExtended learning is facilitated by online materials, including recorded lectures and animated videos of device working principles.

Assignment (CA1)

  • Carry out a Technology Computer-Aided Design (TCAD) assignment on semiconductor device simulation, choosing from a list of assignment topics provided.
  • Tutors guide students in learning the TCAD software during tutorials. A manual is provided for each simulation topic, containing instructions to access the simulation program, problems to be solved and questions to be answered.
  • Students may work in pairs on the same topic; however, each student must submit an individual report, and reports within a pair cannot be identical.
  • After report submission, each student gives a 20-minute oral presentation. A joint presentation by a pair is allowed, with 30 minutes given.
  • The report and presentation are assessed according to the assignment rubrics provided.

Formative Feedback

  • Feedback through discussion within tutorial lessons.
  • Oral feedback on the assignment report and presentation during the Q&A session of the oral presentation.
  • Feedback after each term test on common mistakes and the level of difficulty of the problems.
  • Past examination questions and previous examiner's reports are discussed in lectures.

Course Policies

Academic Integrity. Students are expected to uphold the NTU Honour Code and avoid all forms of academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, academic fraud, collusion and cheating. On the use of technological tools such as Generative AI, refer to the specific assignment instructions or consult the instructor.

Attendance. Students are encouraged to attend from the start of the course. Although lectures are recorded, later lectures build largely on the understanding of content taught earlier, so regular attendance at lectures and lab demonstrations is helpful.

Absence. If you are unable to attend a class (particularly a midterm test) due to illness, email the instructor and submit the medical certificate, issued in Singapore by a registered medical practitioner, to the administrator. A make-up test is conducted only for students absent from both midterm tests with valid reasons.

Diversity and Inclusion. The course aims to provide an inclusive and collaborative learning environment. If your learning is being affected by any circumstance, please speak to the teaching team or the school pastoral officer. All members of the class are expected to respect one another and adhere to the NTU anti-harassment policy.
